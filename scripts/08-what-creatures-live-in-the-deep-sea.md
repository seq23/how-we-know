## Direct-answer lock

The deep sea contains fishes, squid, octopuses, jellies, comb jellies, crustaceans, worms, sea cucumbers, corals, sponges, microbes, and many other groups. Different communities occupy open water, seafloor plains, trenches, vents, seeps, and animal falls.

### The deep sea is many habitats, not one empty place

{{stat: 200 | METRES | Open water below 200 metres covers an enormous volume}}
Open water below 200 meters covers an enormous volume, while the seafloor includes continental slopes, canyons, abyssal plains, seamounts and trenches. Hydrothermal vents, cold seeps and whale falls create concentrated islands of food and chemical energy within the broader food-poor deep ocean.

### Twilight-zone animals

{{zones: TWILIGHT}}
Lanternfishes, bristlemouths, dragonfishes, squid, krill, siphonophores, jellies and zooplankton are common members of the mesopelagic community. Many rise toward the surface at night and return to depth before daylight.

### Midnight and abyssal animals

Anglerfishes, rattails, deep-sea squid, octopuses, sea cucumbers, amphipods, sponges, corals and gelatinous animals occupy the dark water column and seafloor.

### Hadal trench animals

{{stat: 8,400 | METRES | Fish appear to have a physiological depth limit near 8,400 metres}}
Trench communities include amphipods, sea cucumbers, worms, microbes and specialized snailfishes in the shallower portion of the hadal zone. Fish appear to have a physiological depth limit near 8,400 meters, while invertebrates and microbes continue deeper.

### Common myths and questions

{{contrast: SHARKS IN THE DEEP SEA | is=Several shark species that use deep water | not=Present at the greatest trench depths}}
A common question is: Do sharks live in the deep sea? Yes. Several shark species use deep water, although sharks are not present at the greatest trench depths.

{{contrast: WHAT DEEP ECOSYSTEMS RUN ON | is=Food sinking from above | is=Chemosynthesis | not=Photosynthetic plants, which cannot grow where sunlight is absent}}
A common question is: Are there plants in the deep sea? Photosynthetic plants cannot grow where sunlight is absent. Deep ecosystems rely on food sinking from above or on chemosynthesis.

{{define: Bristlemouths | Often described as extraordinarily abundant | They may be the most numerous vertebrates on Earth}}
A common question is: What is the most common deep-sea fish? Bristlemouths are often described as extraordinarily abundant and may be the most numerous vertebrates on Earth.

{{checklist: HOW MANY DEEP-SEA SPECIES ARE UNDISCOVERED? | -No reliable exact count exists | ?New species continue to be documented | ?So do previously unseen behaviors}}
A common question is: How many deep-sea species are undiscovered? No reliable exact count exists. New species and previously unseen behaviors continue to be documented.

### A tour organized by habitat, not celebrity

{{sources: TWO ALMOST UNRELATED PROBLEMS | Open water=a three-dimensional world with few places to hide | The bottom=mud, rock, coral, vents, seeps, carcasses and trench walls}}
A useful inventory of deep-sea life starts by separating the water column from the seafloor. In open water, animals must manage a three-dimensional world with few places to hide. Lanternfish, jellies, siphonophores, squid, crustaceans, and many small migrators move through layers of light and darkness. On the bottom, the habitat changes again. Mud, rock, coral, vents, seeps, carcasses, and trench walls support different communities. A sea cucumber processing sediment and a dragonfish hunting in midwater are both deep-sea animals, but they solve almost unrelated problems.

{{zones:}}
Depth zones are useful labels, not sealed rooms. Animals migrate across boundaries. Food falls through them. Currents transport particles and larvae. A whale carcass can connect surface productivity to the abyss. Hydrothermal vents can support food webs based on chemical energy, while most of the deep ocean still depends ultimately on material produced above. Trench communities occupy steep, isolated basins rather than one continuous hadal plain. This is why a single creature list always feels incomplete. The correct mental model is a network of habitats connected vertically and horizontally.

### The missing majority

{{checklist: WHAT A TWENTY-CREATURE LIST LEAVES OUT | -Microbes, which drive chemical transformations | -Tiny crustaceans, which dominate many samples | -Delicate gelatinous animals that nets destroy | -Burrowing animals invisible to cameras | -Parasites and symbionts}}
Any twenty-creature list will still leave out most of the deep ocean. Microbes drive chemical transformations. Tiny crustaceans dominate many samples. Delicate gelatinous animals are underrepresented when nets destroy them. Burrowing animals can remain invisible to cameras. Parasites and symbionts add relationships that a species list misses entirely. The video should acknowledge that bias instead of using a handful of charismatic animals as the whole ecosystem.

{{sources: EACH METHOD SEES A DIFFERENT OCEAN | A net=catches sturdy bodies | An ROV=records behavior in a narrow lighted field | Acoustics=reveal movement at scale | Environmental DNA=detects traces without showing the animal}}
One short sequence can compare methods: a net catches sturdy bodies, an ROV records behavior in a narrow lighted field, acoustics reveal movement at scale, and environmental DNA detects traces without showing the animal itself. Each method sees a different ocean. The list is therefore an invitation into the habitats, not a census. That distinction matters for future automation too: new episodes should expand underrepresented roles and methods, not just repeat the creatures with the strongest thumbnails.

### What to notice in the edit

Use a map-like journey with a persistent depth and habitat label. Avoid rapid-fire stock montages that imply every animal shares the same water. A siphonophore in open midwater, a coral on a seamount, and a snailfish in a trench belong to different scenes. The viewer should leave with a mental map, not just twenty disconnected names.

### Evidence limit

{{define: Lives in the deep sea | A permanent resident, a daily migrant, a life stage, or an animal that ranges across zones | No short episode can inventory the deep sea, and sampling is uneven across oceans and habitats}}
No short episode can inventory the deep sea. Many species remain undescribed, and sampling is uneven across oceans and habitats. 'Lives in the deep sea' can mean a permanent resident, a daily migrant, a life stage, or an animal that ranges across zones. The script should use examples, not imply a complete census or fixed universal community.
