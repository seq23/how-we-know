## Direct-answer lock

Yeti crabs are deep-sea crustaceans in the family Kiwaidae. Some grow chemosynthetic bacteria on hair-like setae and harvest those bacteria as food near vents or methane seeps.

### A family science met only recently

{{timeline: 2005=First yeti crab described | 2011=Kiwa puravida described}}
The first described yeti crab appeared in scientific literature in 2005, collected near hydrothermal vents in the South Pacific. The animal was pale, with long appendages covered in dense hair-like setae. Researchers placed it in a new family, Kiwaidae. Since then, additional species have been found at vents and methane seeps, including dense populations in Antarctic waters.

{{contrast: THE NICKNAME 'YETI CRAB' | is=A family of animals with different body proportions | is=Different arrangements of setae | not=One species}}
The nickname yeti crab makes them sound like one species, but the family contains animals with different body proportions and different arrangements of setae. Some carry bacteria mainly on claws; others cultivate them across the underside of the body.

{{chain: WHERE THE ENERGY COMES FROM | Chemical energy enters the ecosystem from the seafloor | Microbes use compounds such as hydrogen sulfide or methane-related products | >Carbon is fixed without sunlight}}
The crabs live where chemical energy enters the ecosystem from the seafloor. Microbes can use compounds such as hydrogen sulfide or methane-related products to fix carbon without sunlight. The first open loop is whether the bacteria are merely passengers on the crab or a crop the animal actively encourages.

### The dancing behavior that suggested cultivation

{{descent: 1040 | A METHANE SEEP OFF COSTA RICA}}
During dives at a methane seep off Costa Rica, researchers observed a new yeti crab species rhythmically waving its long, bacteria-covered claws through the escaping fluid. They named it Kiwa puravida.

{{chain: WHY THE WAVING IS NOT RANDOM | The motion was too consistent to dismiss as random current response | Fluid near the seep carries reduced chemicals | Waving may thin the boundary layer around the bacteria | >Improving access to those compounds}}
The motion was too consistent to dismiss as random current response. Fluid near the seep carries reduced chemicals that chemosynthetic bacteria can use. Waving may thin the boundary layer around the bacteria and improve access to those compounds. The crab was not sweeping free bacteria into its mouth. It was moving its own attached microbial community through a better growth environment.

{{contrast: THE TERM FARMING | is=Useful, because the crab improves production of a food source attached to its body | not=Foresight | not=Planting | not=Ownership in a human sense}}
That behavior inspired the term farming. The word is useful because the crab appears to improve production of a food source attached to its body. It can also mislead if it suggests foresight, planting, or ownership in a human sense. The next loop is evidence: how did researchers show the microbes were food rather than camouflage or waste-processing partners?

### Chemistry, anatomy, and behavior point to the same meal

{{ambient}}
{{steps: THREE LINES OF EVIDENCE | Lipid and stable-isotope patterns=the epibiotic bacteria were the crab's main food source | Specialized mouth appendages=capable of scraping bacteria from the setae | Video=showed the arm-waving behavior in seep fluid | >No single observation carries the whole claim}}
The 2011 PLOS ONE study combined several lines of evidence. Lipid and stable-isotope patterns indicated that the epibiotic bacteria were the crab's main food source. The crab possessed specialized mouth appendages capable of scraping bacteria from the setae and transferring them toward the mouth. Video showed the arm-waving behavior in seep fluid. No single observation carries the whole claim.

{{contrast: WHAT ONE OBSERVATION ALONE COULD MEAN | is=Bacteria used for detoxification | is=A scraping appendage used for grooming | is=Waving as courtship or competition | not=Proof of farming on its own}}
A crab with bacteria on its arms might be using them for detoxification. A scraping appendage might serve grooming. Waving might be courtship or competition. When chemistry, morphology, and repeated feeding-related behavior align, the farming explanation becomes much stronger. The study even documented the crab harvesting its bacterial coating. That evidence makes the story more extraordinary than a vague headline, because the mechanism is visible. The unresolved question is how much this strategy varies across the yeti-crab family.

### One family, several microbial arrangements

Not every Kiwa species has been studied with the same methods. Antarctic species cluster around vent flows and carry thick bacterial growth on the underside.

### From strange dance to tested diet

{{timeline: 2005=Kiwaidae named | 2011=Peer-reviewed description of Kiwa puravida}}
Researchers described Kiwa puravida in a 2011 peer-reviewed paper. The crab waves bacteria-covered chelipeds in fluid escaping from a methane seep.

{{steps: WHAT THE AUTHORS TESTED | Lipid analyses | Isotope analyses | Specialized setae used to harvest the bacteria | >Behavior, anatomy and chemistry point in the same direction}}
The authors tested whether those epibiotic bacteria were a main food source using lipid and isotope analyses, and they described specialized setae used to harvest the bacteria. The behavior, anatomy, and chemistry point in the same direction. The word farming remains an interpretation, but it is not based on appearance alone.

### What makes farming the cautious word

{{chain: WHY FARMING IS THE CAUTIOUS WORD | Repeated waving brings bacteria-covered setae into flowing, chemically rich water | Isotope and lipid evidence supports those bacteria as an important food source | The crab scrapes material from the setae toward its mouth | >A useful shorthand that can still imply planning the evidence does not require}}
{{text}}
The crab's repeated waving brings bacteria-covered setae into contact with flowing, chemically rich water. Stable-isotope and lipid evidence supports those bacteria as an important food source. The crab also scrapes material from the setae toward its mouth. Together, those observations make cultivation or farming a useful shorthand. But the word can imply planning and ownership that the evidence does not require.
