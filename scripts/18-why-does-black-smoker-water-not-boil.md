Black-smoker fluid can exceed 340°C without boiling because immense seafloor pressure raises water's boiling point. Minerals precipitate when the hot fluid mixes with cold seawater.

### One hundred degrees is not a universal boiling point

{{pressure: 3000}}
The familiar boiling point of water, one hundred degrees Celsius, belongs to a specific pressure: roughly one atmosphere at sea level.

{{chain: WHY PRESSURE MOVES THE BOILING POINT | Boiling begins when vapor bubbles can exist against the surrounding pressure | Increase that pressure | >The temperature required for stable vapor rises}}
Boiling begins when vapor bubbles can exist against the surrounding pressure. Increase that pressure and the temperature required for stable vapor rises.

{{chain: WHY THE FLUID STAYS LIQUID | Hydrothermal vents lie beneath kilometers of seawater | The surrounding pressure is hundreds of atmospheres | Vent fluid can exceed 340 degrees Celsius | >The fluid stays dense and liquid-like instead of flashing into steam}}
Hydrothermal vents lie beneath kilometers of seawater. At two or three thousand meters, the surrounding pressure is hundreds of atmospheres. NOAA reports vent fluid above 340 degrees Celsius, and some measured systems are hotter. Under that pressure the fluid can remain dense and liquid-like instead of flashing into steam the way it would in an open pot.

{{checklist: WHAT ELSE ALTERS THE PHASE BEHAVIOR | ?Salts | ?Dissolved chemicals | >The vent is not a pipe connected to magma}}
Salts and dissolved chemicals alter the phase behavior further. The first open loop is origin: the vent is not a pipe connected to magma. The fluid usually begins as ordinary seawater entering fractured ocean crust.

### A circulation system inside the crust

{{chain: A CIRCULATION SYSTEM INSIDE THE CRUST | Seawater percolates down through cracks | It warms and reacts chemically in hot rock | Metals and sulfur-bearing compounds enter solution | The heated fluid becomes buoyant and rises through fractures | >It exits the seafloor, and the chimney is built by the fluid it channels}}
{{text}}
Seawater percolates down through cracks near spreading centers and volcanic regions. As it travels through hot rock, it warms and reacts chemically.

Magnesium can be removed. Metals and sulfur-bearing compounds enter solution. The heated fluid becomes buoyant and rises through fractures until it exits the seafloor.

The route can extend through a complex subsurface network, so temperature and chemistry differ from vent to vent. The chimney is built by the fluid it channels.

{{contrast: A BLACK SMOKER | is=A hydrothermal circulation outlet | not=A miniature underwater volcano ejecting ash | not=A pipe connected to magma}}
This is why a black smoker is not a miniature underwater volcano ejecting ash. It is a hydrothermal circulation outlet. The pressure keeps the fluid from ordinary boiling, while the rock-water reactions load it with dissolved material. The next loop begins the instant that clear, hot, mineral-rich fluid meets near-freezing seawater and appears to turn black.

### The smoke is a mineral cloud

{{chain: WHY THE PLUME LOOKS BLACK | Hot fluid mixes with cold, oxygenated seawater | Dissolved metals precipitate | Tiny metal-sulfide particles scatter light like smoke | >They settle onto chimney surfaces and build structures}}
{{ambient}}
The dark plume consists largely of tiny metal-sulfide particles. When the hot fluid mixes with cold, oxygenated seawater, dissolved metals precipitate. The particles scatter light like smoke and settle onto chimney surfaces, building structures that can grow, branch, collapse, and reform.

{{contrast: BLACK AND WHITE SMOKERS | is=Black smokers are commonly rich in iron sulfides | is=White smokers carry different mineral mixtures | not=Combustion | not=Anything that needs oxygen to burn}}
Black smokers are commonly rich in iron sulfides; white smokers carry different mineral mixtures that produce a lighter appearance.

{{define: Smoker | Visual shorthand | There is no combustion, and nothing needs oxygen to burn}}
There is no combustion. Nothing needs oxygen to burn. The word smoker is visual shorthand. Chemistry also changes rapidly across centimeters.

Fluid near the outlet may be lethal to most animals, while a short distance away mixing lowers temperature and supplies both reduced chemicals and oxygen.

### Boiling is a pressure question

{{stat: 100 | DEGREES CELSIUS | The boiling point of water at one atmosphere, at sea level}}
{{magnitude: BOILING POINT AND VENT FLUID | degrees Celsius | Water at sea level=100 | Vent fluid can exceed=340}}
At sea level, water boils near 100 degrees Celsius because its vapor pressure can match the surrounding atmosphere. Deep underwater, the surrounding pressure is much greater, so liquid water can remain stable at far higher temperatures. Seawater moves through cracks, is heated near magma, reacts with rock, and rises. When the hot fluid meets near-freezing seawater, dissolved minerals precipitate into dark particles and build black-smoker chimneys.
