His idea was that what you quietly assume to be true about yourself, and about your life, tends to gently unfold. It tends to show up in how you experience your days. It becomes part of your quiet reality.

To assume something means to take it as already true. You accept it in your mind, in your heart, as if it is already here. You do this even before you can see any evidence of it in your outer world.

It is like a soft, inner knowing. A feeling of quiet certainty. You gently settle into the idea that this chosen thing is already so. You let it feel real within you.

This practice is about choosing an assumption that feels good to you. You pick something you would love to experience. Something you would love to feel true about yourself, or about your life.

Perhaps it is a feeling of deep peace. Or a sense of quiet well-being. Maybe it is the feeling of being completely supported. You simply choose one gentle idea.

Then, the practice is to keep returning to that feeling. You bring your soft attention back to it, over and over. You do this calmly, without effort or strain.

You simply let your awareness drift back to your chosen assumption. You let yourself feel the quiet truth of it. You allow that feeling to gently fill your inner space.
