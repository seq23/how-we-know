Imagine judging a submarine by the rules of a bicycle. That is roughly what happens when we judge a deep-sea animal by the rules of daylight. The animal is not badly designed. We are looking at a body optimized for a world we almost never experience. Deep-sea creatures look unusual because darkness, crushing pressure, cold, and scarce food reward body plans that would be unnecessary near the surface—large mouths, light organs, soft tissues, unusual eyes, and energy-saving movement. The useful question is not “Why is this creature weird?” It is “What problem does this feature solve?” Once you ask that, the huge mouth, transparent head, glowing lure, soft tissue, and needle-like teeth stop looking random.
