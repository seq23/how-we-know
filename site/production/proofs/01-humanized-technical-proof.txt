That fish is not deformed. The photograph is what happens when an animal built for darkness meets our daylight, our camera angle, and our idea of what a normal body should look like. The useful question is not why deep-sea creatures are weird. It is what problem each feature solves.

 I used to look at deep-sea animals as a collection of freaks. The source material changed that. Once I matched each feature to a constraint—darkness, pressure, cold, scarce food—the transparent head and oversized mouth stopped looking random and started looking precise.

There is no single adaptation called a deep-sea body plan, and 'weird' is not a scientific category.
