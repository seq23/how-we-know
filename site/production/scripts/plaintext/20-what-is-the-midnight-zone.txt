What is the midnight zone?

Ocean zones are useful boundaries, not physical walls. NOAA places the midnight, or bathypelagic, zone between roughly 1,000 and 4,000 meters. Above it, the twilight zone retains faint surface light. Below about 1,000 meters, sunlight is effectively absent. Below 4,000 meters, the abyssal zone begins in the common five-zone scheme. The seafloor may intersect any of these depth bands depending on local topography, so midnight zone does not automatically mean bottom. Over a trench, it is open water far above the floor. Along a continental slope, it may include benthic habitat. The zone is cold, under high pressure, and disconnected from direct photosynthesis. Yet it is not empty. Fishes, squids, jellies, crustaceans, microbes, and drifting gelatinous animals occupy the water.
