# POV interview 3 — the domains that are not deep sea

**Source file when you answer:** `pov/answers-3.txt` — number each answer to match.
Speak it, don't write it. Dictate into your phone if that's faster; the messy
spoken version is better raw material than a tidy written one.

**Why this exists.** `tier: specific` POV lines don't transfer between domains —
a line about black water says nothing about a silicon wafer. Deep sea has 31 of
them. Materials currently has 3, and they were written for it and approved by
you rather than drawn from anything you said. Every other domain has zero, which
means the day the system promotes one it has no voice for it.

This interview covers **eleven domains at once**, in ranked order of demand, so
one sitting stocks the bank for years instead of one niche.

**Rules**
- No answer needs to be correct. A POV line is a *stance*, not a fact — the facts
  come from cited sources, and a wrong opinion is still yours.
- Concrete beats abstract. "It looks like a mistake until you know the pressure"
  is usable. "Science is fascinating" is not.
- Two to four sentences each. If a question does nothing for you, say **skip** and
  move on — a forced answer is worse than a missing one.
- Say what you actually think, including the unflattering version. The lines that
  land are the ones that sound like a person rather than a narrator.

---

## Part 1 — Stance (≈8 min, produces `tier: transferable`)

These are the highest-value answers in the whole document. A transferable line
works in *any* evidence-based niche, so these cover every domain below and every
domain not invented yet.

1. When you find out something you believed was flatly wrong, what does that
   actually feel like? Not "humbling" — describe the real reaction.

2. What kind of explanation makes you stop trusting the person giving it?

3. You run this channel on the rule that every number is traceable to a named
   public source. What made you insist on that, specifically?

4. Where do you notice people confidently describing something they've obviously
   never touched? Any field.

5. What's a thing you find genuinely beautiful that most people would call
   boring or ugly?

6. When you look at something engineered — a bridge, a chip, a ship — what do
   you find yourself wondering about that other people don't ask?

7. What's the difference between something that's *complicated* and something
   that's *hard*? Use an example from your own work if one comes.

8. Finish this honestly: "The thing that gets me about any of this is —"

## Part 2 — Domains (≈12 min, produces `tier: specific`)

Two questions each, in the order the system would promote them. **Answer the
ones that spark and skip the rest** — six domains answered well beats eleven
answered thinly. Say the domain number before you answer so it files correctly.

### 9 · Space and astronomy *(top-ranked, demand 0.74)*
9a. What's your honest reaction to a distance like four light-years — does the
number mean anything to you, or does it slide off?
9b. Is there anything about space that you find *unsettling* rather than
inspiring?

### 10 · Physics fundamentals *(0.74)*
10a. Which piece of basic physics did school make boring that you'd defend now?
10b. What's something that behaves in a way that still feels wrong to you even
though you know it's true?

### 11 · Materials and manufacturing *(0.66 — currently running)*
11a. What goes through your head watching something be *made* — glass, steel, a
circuit board?
11b. Everything eventually fails: metal fatigues, glass shatters, plastic goes
brittle. Does that read to you as a flaw or as normal?
11c. Titanium, graphene, Damascus steel — materials get talked about like magic.
What's your reaction to that kind of hype?

### 12 · Paleontology and extinction *(0.44)*
12a. Deep time — hundreds of millions of years. Can you hold that, or is it just
a word?
12b. What's your feeling about a species that existed for fifty million years and
left almost no trace?

### 13 · Incident analysis *(0.43 — how things go wrong)*
13a. You've worked in and around organisations. When something fails badly, where
do you actually look first?
13b. What's your view on blaming the person at the controls?

### 14 · Infrastructure and megaprojects *(0.41)*
14a. Tunnels, dams, grids — what's your reaction to the scale of something like
that being *normal* to the people who built it?
14b. What infrastructure do you rely on daily and understand least?

### 15 · Logistics — how things move *(0.38)*
15a. A container arrives on time from the other side of the planet. Is that
impressive to you or invisible?
15b. Where have you personally watched a supply chain break?

### 16 · Earth science, geology, volcanoes *(0.35)*
16a. What's your reaction to living on something that is actively moving?
16b. Is there a landscape that changed how you thought about time?

### 17 · Measurement and dating *(0.35 — how we know how old things are)*
17a. This one is almost the channel's name. What convinces you that a number
about the distant past is real?
17b. Where do you think measurement gets oversold?

### 18 · Weather and storms *(0.34)*
18a. What's the closest you've been to weather you couldn't argue with?
18b. Forecasting is probabilistic and people hate that. Where do you land?

### 19 · Energy and power *(0.29)*
19a. What's your honest position on how electricity actually reaches you?
19b. Which energy argument do you think is being had dishonestly?

## Part 3 — Two closers (≈2 min)

20. If someone watches forty of these videos, what should they end up believing
about how knowledge gets made?

21. What would make you want to stop making this channel? Answer it straight —
the negative version of a stance is often the most usable line in a bank.
